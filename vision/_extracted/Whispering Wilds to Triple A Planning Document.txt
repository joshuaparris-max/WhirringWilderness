# Whispering Wilds – Vision v1.0
## High Concept
**One-sentence vision**
> Whispering Wilds is a slow-burn, text-first wildlands RPG about listening to a haunted forest, where every step, word, and choice bends the fate of its spirits and settlements.
**Elevator pitch**
You wake in a small sanctum on the edge of an ancient forest that remembers everything.  
Through text, choices, and light tactical encounters, you explore a living wildland: walking its trails, listening to its whispers, meeting keepers and wanderers, and slowly deciding whether this place is healed, exploited, or left to fade.
The game is **text-first** (words do most of the work), supported by **evocative art**, **ambient sound**, and a **simple, tactile UI**.
---
## What the Game Is
- **Text-first wildlands RPG**
  - Exploration driven by descriptions, dialogue, and choices.
  - You navigate Sanctum ? Gate ? Wilds ? Lake / Mine / Hermit’s Hut / Trader’s Post and beyond.
  - The forest feels like a character, not just a backdrop.
- **Slow-burn, cozy-haunted tone**
  - Quiet, contemplative, a bit eerie.
  - No cheap jump scares; tension comes from mood, implication, and consequence.
  - The player should feel “held but uneasy” rather than constantly under attack.
- **Focused design pillars**
  1. **Presence in the Wild**
     - Movement, weather, light, and ambient events matter.
     - Short, sensory text reinforces that each step has weight.
     - Returning to familiar locations feels like coming back to a place with memory.
  2. **Relational Storytelling**
     - Core NPCs (Caretaker, Hermit, Ranger/Trader) anchor the story.
     - Dialogue and choices shape trust, access, and how the forest responds.
     - Spiritual/ethical questions are present but not preachy; players feel invited to care.
  3. **Light Survival & Crafting**
     - Simple loops: gather ? craft ? restore/prepare ? push further.
     - Systems (forage, mine, fish, craft) support the story of tending and surviving, not grind.
     - Managing risk vs reward (go deeper vs turn back to Sanctum) is a core tension.
- **Progression and structure**
  - Level 1–5 in the vertical slice (later higher).
  - Small skill/attribute bumps, new options in exploration/combat.
  - Quests like **Heal the Grove**, **Echoes at the Lake**, **Hermit’s Glow** form Act I.
  - A short “evening session” structure: 30–90 minutes feels like a complete chapter.
- **Presentation**
  - Main layout: **text log on the left**, **art on the right**, **choices along the bottom/side**.
  - Background art per key location; portraits for major NPCs.
  - Ambient audio per biome + light UI/feedback sounds.
  - Accessible defaults: readable fonts, text-size options, dark theme.
---
## What the Game Is Not
- **Not a twitch action game**
  - No real-time dodging, combo meters, or high APM demands.
  - Combat is turn-based and readable, with few but meaningful choices.
- **Not an endless open-world sandbox**
  - The world is curated, handcrafted, and finite.
  - Quality of locations and encounters beats raw map size.
- **Not a loot treadmill or min-max cruncher**
  - Items and gear matter, but they serve story and survival, not build spreadsheets.
  - No 200-item inventory of marginal stat upgrades.
- **Not a hardcore survival sim**
  - No complex hunger/thirst micromanagement or harsh punishment loops.
  - Resource pressure exists but is gentle and in service of mood and pacing.
- **Not grimdark horror**
  - The forest can be scary, but the core is wonder, lament, and healing.
  - No torture porn, no shock-for-shock’s-sake content.
---
## Target Player
Whispering Wilds is for players who:
- Enjoy **reading** and sitting with atmosphere.
- Like wandering slowly through spaces, piecing together **lore and relationships**.
- Prefer **meaningful choices** over fast reflexes.
- Appreciate **“small but deep” games** they can play in evenings: 30–90 minutes per session.
- Are drawn to:
  - Quiet, reflective, sometimes spiritual themes.
  - Nature, mystery, and the feeling of “this place is watching me too”.
  - Games where **kindness, curiosity, and restraint** are valid playstyles.
They might be:
- Fans of narrative/indie games on Steam and itch.
- Former tabletop/D&D players who like strong prose and worldbuilding.
- People who loved text adventures / MUDs and want a modern, curated version.
---

Whispering Wilds is not trying to copy these games directly, but to stand alongside them as a **distinctly text-first, cozy-haunted wildlands RPG** with its own voice.

# Whispering Wilds – Design Pillars
These pillars are the non-negotiables that guide every decision in Whispering Wilds.  
If a feature, scene, or system clashes with these, it gets changed or cut.
---
## Pillar 1 – Presence in the Wild
The forest is the main character. Every step, pause, and detour should deepen the player’s sense that they are moving through a living, watching wildland. Text, pacing, sound, and visuals all work together to create a quiet, immersive awareness of place: weather shifting, light changing, paths opening and closing, and the sense that the Wilds remember where you’ve been and how you’ve treated them.
### Do
- Describe **small sensory details** regularly: rustling leaves, mist on skin, distant calls, the smell of wet stone or ash.
- Let **weather, time of day, and ambient events** subtly change descriptions and options in familiar locations.
- Use **returning to known places** (Sanctum, Gate, Wilds crossroads) to reinforce that the world persists and remembers.
- Make **travel decisions** feel meaningful: “push deeper” vs “circle back” vs “wait and listen”.
- Tie **environment changes** (a healed grove, a polluted lake, a cleared path) to player choices and quest outcomes.
### Don’t
- Don’t spam long, purple-prose paragraphs every move; **keep descriptions tight and focused**.
- Don’t treat locations as static backdrops with no change after quests or key events.
- Don’t reduce the Wilds to pure mechanics (“+2 wood, +3 ore”) with no sensory or emotional framing.
- Don’t jump-cut the player all over the map with instant fast travel early on; preserve **journey and distance**.
- Don’t drown the player in map UI noise; the **feel of moving** matters more than hyper-detailed cartography.
---
## Pillar 2 – Relational Storytelling
The heart of the game is not just what you do, but **who you listen to and how you treat them**. NPCs like the Caretaker, Hermit, and Ranger/Trader, along with spirits and settlements, are lenses through which the player understands the Wilds. Choices in dialogue, gifts, and actions change trust, access, and tone. The player should feel that relationships—human and non-human—are the real stakes.
### Do
- Give each core NPC a **clear voice, worldview, and wound** that shows up consistently in dialogue.
- Let **choices in conversation and action** change how characters respond, what they reveal, and what they’ll risk for you.
- Use **small relational beats** (shared tea, quiet prayers, awkward silences) alongside big plot moments.
- Reflect the player’s **reputation and past actions** in how NPCs describe the forest, your deeds, and each other.
- Let spiritual and ethical questions emerge through **relationships and situations**, not lectures.
### Don’t
- Don’t reduce NPCs to pure quest dispensers (“fetch X, kill Y”) with no personality or follow-through.
- Don’t make choices feel fake; avoid giving three options that all lead to the same outcome and tone.
- Don’t info-dump lore in giant unbroken blocks; **weave it into dialogue, locations, items, and memories**.
- Don’t treat spirits or the forest as generic “magic”; give them **motives, history, and relational tension**.
- Don’t undercut serious or tender moments with constant sarcasm or meta jokes.
---
## Pillar 3 – Light Survival & Crafting
Survival and crafting exist to **support the mood and story**, not to become a second job. Gathering, fishing, mining, and crafting should feel like tending and preparing: making salves for a grove, mending gear before a deeper push, trading fairly with the Ranger/Trader. The systems should be simple, readable, and gently press the player to balance risk and rest.
### Do
- Keep resource systems **small and legible** (a handful of meaningful materials, not dozens of near-duplicates).
- Use gathering and crafting to **unlock story and environmental change** (healed groves, lit paths, calmed spirits).
- Make **risk vs reward** clear: going deeper with low supplies should feel tense, not random or unfair.
- Provide **tools to prepare well** (resting at Sanctum, simple upgrades, crafted remedies) before harder pushes.
- Let crafted items and resources show up in **narrative moments** (“the salve stings, but the bark brightens”).
### Don’t
- Don’t turn gathering into endless grinding with tiny drop rates and no narrative payoff.
- Don’t bury players in crafting recipes with tiny, incremental stat bumps; **avoid spreadsheet gameplay**.
- Don’t add hardcore meters (thirst, temperature, intricate nutrition) that distract from exploration and story.
- Don’t punish experimentation harshly; mistakes should teach and nudge, not wipe hours of progress.
- Don’t let survival overshadow relationships; **if a choice is between “more numbers” and “better story”, choose story**.

# Whispering Wilds – Core Loop (Vertical Slice)
## 1. Five-Minute Loop (Narrative)
A typical 5–10 minute session chunk in the vertical slice looks like this:
1. **Prepare in Sanctum**
   - The player checks their current quest, glances at supplies (salves, food, tools), maybe has a brief word with the Caretaker.
   - They choose a direction/purpose: “push toward the Lake”, “check the Mine”, or “visit the Hermit/Trader”.
2. **Step through the Gate into the Wilds**
   - The player leaves the safety of Sanctum and passes through the Gate.
   - The first descriptions emphasise weather, mood, and any lingering effects of past actions (healed grove, changed paths, etc.).
3. **Explore and Sense**
   - In the Wilds, the player:
     - Moves a step or two into the chosen region (toward Lake / Mine / Hut / Post).
     - Uses **sense/listen/observe** to get a feel for the area: sounds, traces, spirits, signs of danger.
   - The forest “answers” with short, evocative text and sometimes hints about resources or threats.
4. **Gather / Risk**
   - Based on what they sense, the player:
     - **Forages**, **fishes**, or **mines** in a location, OR
     - Chooses to **move cautiously** deeper instead.
   - Gathering can:
     - Yield useful resources for quests/crafting, and/or
     - Trigger a **creature encounter** or other danger.
5. **Encounter / Consequence**
   - If a creature appears or an event triggers:
     - The player makes a small set of tactical choices in a **turn-based encounter** (fight, use item, attempt to escape, etc.).
     - Outcome grants **XP**, possibly loot, and may set or clear narrative flags (e.g. “you’ve angered something in the Lake”).
   - Even without combat, the forest may respond (a new path opens, a spirit stirs, the weather shifts).
6. **Decide: Return or Push Deeper**
   - After a couple of actions and maybe an encounter, the player chooses:
     - **Return toward Sanctum** to bank progress, rest, and advance quests, OR
     - **Press deeper** for more reward and risk, with dwindling resources/HP.
7. **Progress a Quest / Relationship**
   - Back at Sanctum, the Lake, Mine, Hermit’s Hut, or Trader’s Post:
     - The player hands in items, shares news, or responds to NPC questions.
     - A quest step advances, relationship text updates, and the Wilds may subtly change.
   - The player receives:
     - XP, maybe a level-up,
     - New dialogue options,
     - A new lead or objective for the next foray.
Then the loop begins again: **prepare ? step out ? sense ? gather/risk ? encounter ? decide ? progress**.

**States (Nodes)**
1. **Sanctum (Prepare)**
   - Safe hub: check quests, talk to Caretaker, manage inventory, plan route.
2. **Gate (Threshold)**
   - Emotional/visual transition from safety to Wilds.
   - Quick reminders of current conditions (weather, time, forest mood).
3. **Exploration Node (Wilds / Lake / Mine / Hut / Post)**
   - Player can move, sense, gather, and trigger events.
   - Each region is a cluster of exploration nodes.
4. **Gather/Action Node**
   - Specific action (forage/mine/fish/listen/pray/etc.) taken in a location.
   - Has a chance to:
     - Succeed quietly,
     - Trigger an **Encounter Node**,
     - Set a story flag.
5. **Encounter Node (Creature/Event)**
   - Turn-based conflict or event resolution.
   - Leads to:
     - Reward (XP, items),
     - Cost (HP/resource loss),
     - Flag changes (e.g. “spirit calmed”, “something angered”).
6. **Decision Node (Return vs Push Deeper)**
   - Based on current HP, supplies, and quest urgency.
   - Branches:
     - **Return Path** ? Gate ? Sanctum.
     - **Deeper Path** ? another Exploration Node in a more dangerous region.
7. **Quest/Relationship Node**
   - Hand-in or story beat with an NPC (Caretaker, Hermit, Ranger/Trader).
   - Advances quest stages and relational states.
**Edges (Transitions)**
- Sanctum ? Gate ? First Exploration Node.
- Exploration Node ? (Move) ? Another Exploration Node.
- Exploration Node ? (Sense/Gather) ? Gather/Action Node.
- Gather/Action Node ? (Trigger) ? Encounter Node.
- Encounter Node ? (Outcome) ? Decision Node.
- Decision Node ? (Return) ? Gate ? Sanctum ? Quest/Relationship Node.
- Decision Node ? (Push Deeper) ? “Deeper” Exploration Node.
- Quest/Relationship Node ? Sanctum (Prepare) for the next loop.
---

# Whispering Wilds – Narrative Scaffold (Act I / Vertical Slice)
## 1. Premise – Sanctum + Wilds Arc
On the ragged edge of an ancient forest stands a small Sanctum: part chapel, part shelter, part watch-post against whatever moves between the trees. You arrive with little memory of why you’ve come, only a pull in your chest and the sense that the Wilds have been waiting. The forest is sick in places and strangely generous in others – groves that won’t grow, a lake that whispers under the wind, a mine that hums with something older than ore. A handful of caretakers, hermits, and traders live in uneasy partnership with the Wilds, each carrying their own griefs and bargains. As you step through the Gate and begin walking the trails, you slowly realise that every small act – listening, healing, taking, returning – bends the fate of both the forest and the scattered lives that depend on it.
---
## 2. Core NPC Snapshots (Act I Cast)
**Caretaker**  
A gentle, worn keeper of the Sanctum who believes the forest can still be healed, even as they hide the cost that hope has already taken on their body and faith.
**Hermit**  
A prickly recluse tucked deep in the Wilds, half-priest and half-witch, who hears the forest more clearly than anyone else and resents you for stirring up what they’ve tried to keep quiet.
**Ranger/Trader**  
A practical wanderer who walks the paths between Sanctum, camp, mine, and lake, trading stories and supplies, loyal to survival first but secretly weighing which side of the forest’s future to back.

## 3. Act I Outline – Vertical Slice Only
### Act I.1 – Waking in the Sanctum (Intro)
**Goal:** Establish tone, core fantasy, and first quest hook.
- **Opening image**
  - You wake in a small room in the Sanctum: stone, timber, candles, the muffled hush of the forest beyond the walls.
  - Short text gently introduces:
    - The idea that the forest “remembers”.
    - The Gate as the threshold.
    - The Caretaker as your first human anchor.
- **First conversation: Caretaker**
  - Learns your name (or lets player choose a simple one).
  - Hints that:
    - The Wilds have been “off” lately – a grove that won’t heal, strange quiet by the lake, a tension in the mine’s depths.
    - They asked for help, and you came (or were sent).
  - Offers your **first quest hook**:
    - “Before anything else, I’d like you to visit the grove. It’s just beyond the Gate. Listen. Tell me what you feel.”
- **Sanctum as hub**
  - Tutorial-like prompts:
    - Check simple inventory (a charm, a worn cloak, maybe a small token).
    - See your first quest in the journal: *Heal the Grove – Step 1: Visit the grove and listen.*
  - The Caretaker nudges you toward the Gate:
    - “Don’t stay inside too long. The forest isn’t fixed by worry.”
### Act I.2 – First Steps Through the Gate (Entering the Wilds)
**Goal:** Make crossing the threshold feel significant and set up the loop.
- **Gate scene**
  - Short, focused description of stepping through:
    - Change in sound, smell, quality of light.
    - Subtle shift of “being watched but not yet judged”.
  - You arrive at the **Wilds crossroads**:
    - Paths signed or implied toward the Grove/Lake/Mine/Hermit/Trader.
    - For now, only the grove path is strongly highlighted.
- **Learn to sense**
  - Tutorial of **sense/listen/observe**:
    - Using it here gives a first glimpse: “The forest holds its breath. Something is waiting near the grove.”
  - This gently teaches:
    - “Before acting, you can always listen first.”
- **Quiet travel**
  - A few short moves along the grove path:
    - Moments of small sensory detail: birds going silent, the smell of sap, soft mist.
    - Chance for a minor, non-lethal encounter or unsettling sign (tracks, an echo, a faint voice) with no full combat yet.
### Act I.3 – The Grove and the First True Choice (Quest: Heal the Grove)
**Goal:** Introduce the forest’s sickness + your role as listener/healer, not just fighter.
- **Arrive at the Grove**
  - The grove is wrong: leaves dull, soil sour, light strangely thin.
  - Using **sense/listen** here:
    - Gives a more intense, almost spoken impression: grief, exhaustion, or a specific memory fragment.
- **First functional task**
  - The forest “suggests” you’ll need something:
    - Herbs from nearby glades,
    - Water taken respectfully from the Lake,
    - Ore or crystals from the Mine to anchor a charm,
    - Or guidance from the Hermit.
  - The quest **splits into steps**:
    - “Gather what the grove needs” – choose which path to follow first (Lake, Mine, Hermit, Trader).
- **Soft choice**
  - The game makes it clear there’s no “perfect” order, but:
    - Going to the Hermit might give more understanding but is emotionally harder.
    - Going to the Lake or Mine may be “easier” but risk angering spirits if you take too much.
### Act I.4 – Meeting the Hermit (Quest: Hermit’s Glow)
**Goal:** Deepen relational storytelling and spiritual/ethical tone.
- **Journey to the Hermit’s Hut**
  - The Wilds feel a bit sharper here, more watchful.
  - A minor creature encounter introduction:
    - Simple combat tutorial with low stakes.
    - After the fight, the forest’s reaction is noted (approval? concern? unease?).
- **Hermit encounter**
  - The Hermit sees you coming and is not impressed:
    - “Another fixer sent from the stone house? The forest is tired of being fixed.”
  - Dialogue reveals:
    - They hear the forest more directly.
    - They believe the grove is sick not just from “bad energy” but from broken promises and extraction.
  - **Hermit’s request (Hermit’s Glow)**
    - They will help, but only if you:
      - Bring them a certain herb from the Wilds and a rare component from the Mine, **without** overharvesting or angering something at the Lake.
    - Optional subtle choice:
      - Accept conditions respectfully,
      - Push back (changes tone, but still allows progress).
### Act I.5 – The Lake, the Mine, and the Trader (Quest: Echoes at the Lake)
**Goal:** Show different facets of the Wilds and introduce the Ranger/Trader.
- **Lake path**
  - The Lake is beautiful but uneasy:
    - Surface too still, whispers under the wind.
  - **Sense/listen** here:
    - You feel layered voices, some grieving, some accusatory.
  - A light **Echoes at the Lake** quest beat:
    - Take water respectfully (with a small ritual / choice of words).
    - Or carelessly (e.g. scooping quickly, ignoring the mood).
    - This sets a flag that will colour how the Lake and some spirits treat you later.
- **Mine path**
  - The Mine hums with old work and older stone.
  - Minor creature encounter or event:
    - Not just “a monster in the dark”: maybe half-spirit, half-insect, confused rather than purely hostile.
  - You gather:
    - Enough ore or crystal for the Hermit’s charm or the grove’s need.
    - How you treat the Mine (take just enough vs strip a bit more) may set another quiet flag.
- **Trader’s Post**
  - On the path or as a small hub:
    - The Ranger/Trader offers supplies, lore, and perspective.
  - Their dialogue:
    - Frames survival pragmatically: “Timber and ore feed bellies too.”
    - Raises tension between healing the grove, feeding people, and keeping trade flowing.
  - They may:
    - Exchange your gathered goods,
    - Offer a crafted tool or charm,
    - Share a rumour about the forest’s deeper trouble (Act II seeds).
### Act I.6 – Healing the Grove (Climax Event)
**Goal:** Pay off the opening quest with a meaningful, not-perfect resolution.
- **Return with gathered components**
  - You bring herbs/water/ore (and possibly Hermit’s chant or charm) back to the Grove.
  - The game checks silent flags:
    - How you treated the Lake, Mine, paths, and Hermit.
- **Ritual / Healing Scene**
  - You perform a simple structured ritual:
    - Choice of words, posture, intention (e.g. “ask permission”, “command change”, “offer apology”).
  - Outcome:
    - The grove is **partially healed**:
      - Colours return, sounds brighten, but a scar remains.
    - The forest responds differently depending on your earlier choices:
      - Grateful, wary, or mixed.
- **Consequences**
  - The Caretaker and Hermit both react:
    - Caretaker sees hope, maybe relief.
    - Hermit sees what you stirred and warns that deeper roots remain.
  - You earn:
    - XP, maybe level up.
    - A small item / blessing.
    - A shift in how at least one NPC speaks to you.
### Act I.7 – “To Be Continued…” Hook
**Goal:** Signal a larger arc while giving closure to the slice.
- **Quiet epilogue moment in Sanctum**
  - Night at Sanctum, the forest softly audible outside.
  - Caretaker or Ranger/Trader hints:
    - The grove was only one wound.
    - There are older scars in the Wilds – something beneath the Mine, beyond the Lake, or upriver.
- **Vision / dream / omen (optional)**
  - A dream-like text scene:
    - Paths branching in the dark.
    - A figure at the edge of the lake, or a light deep beneath the roots.
  - No answers, just an emotional promise: **“There’s more, and it will need you.”**
- **Act I boundary**
  - The player wakes back in Sanctum with:
    - Clear indication that Act I is complete.
    - A new main quest stub (“Follow the call beneath the Mine”, “Trace the echoes upstream”, etc.) marked as **future content**.
  - For the vertical slice, this is the natural stopping point:
    - You’ve met the core NPCs, walked the loop, healed something, and glimpsed the bigger story.

# Whispering Wilds – Vertical Slice Tech Spec (Browser Only)
## 1. Platform & Deployment
**Target platform (vSlice):**
- **Primary:** Modern desktop browsers (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari)
- **Input:** Keyboard + mouse (controller is a “later” feature)
- **Distribution:** 
  - Hosted web build (e.g. Netlify/Vercel/GitHub Pages)
  - Can be wrapped later for desktop (Electron/Tauri) without changing core code
**Performance expectations:**
- Runs smoothly on typical laptops/desktops with no GPU reliance.
- Minimal memory footprint (text-first, light art, simple audio).
---
## 2. Stack Overview
**Language:**
- **TypeScript** (strict mode) for type safety and AI-friendliness.
**Framework / Tooling:**
- **React** for UI (component model, easy Cursor support, huge ecosystem).
- **Vite** for bundling/dev server (fast, simple config).
- **React Router** (optional, light) only if needed for dev/debug views; core game is single-page.
**State Management:**
- Simple, explicit state using:
  - React context + reducers **or**
  - A small state library (e.g. Zustand) if needed for clarity.
- Game logic separated from React components (pure TS modules).
**Audio:**
- Native Web Audio API or a small helper library (e.g. Howler.js) for:
  - Ambient loops
  - UI SFX
  - Short musical stingers
**Styling:**
- CSS Modules or TailwindCSS (pick one and stick to it).
- Focus on legible typography and layout, not heavy animation.
**Testing & Quality:**
- **Vitest** or **Jest** for unit tests on core logic.
- ESLint + Prettier for consistent code style.
---
## 3. High-Level Architecture
### 3.1 Core Modules
1. **Engine Layer (`/src/engine`)**
   Pure TypeScript, no React imports. This holds game rules and core loop.
   - `GameState`  
     - Player stats (HP, XP, level), inventory, flags, current location.
     - Quest states, discovered creatures, relationship flags.
   - `locations.ts`  
     - Data + helpers for Sanctum, Gate, Wilds, Lake, Mine, Hermit’s Hut, Trader’s Post.
     - Defines exits, tags, biome, base descriptions.
   - `actions.ts`  
     - Core verbs: move, sense/observe, gather (forage/mine/fish), rest, talk, use item.
     - Each action takes `GameState` + parameters and returns:
       - New `GameState`
       - A list of `LogEntry` objects (for the UI).
   - `combat.ts`  
     - Turn-based combat loop.
     - Creature stats/abilities.
     - Attack, defend, use item, attempt escape.
     - XP + loot rewards.
 
  - `quests.ts`  
     - Quest definitions (Heal the Grove, Hermit’s Glow, Echoes at the Lake).
     - Functions to advance quest stages based on flags/actions.
   - `narrative_flags.ts`  
     - Encapsulates forest mood, lake/mine treatment, small relational flags.
     - Provides helpers like `describeGrove(state)`.
   - `bestiary.ts`  
     - Creature data and flavour text.
     - Unlock logic for entries.
   - `persistence.ts`  
     - Serialise/deserialise `GameState` to and from JSON.
     - Interface for saving/loading via browser storage.
2. **Content Layer (`/src/content`)**
   Data, not logic. Easy for AI / humans to extend.
   - `creatures.json` / `creatures.ts`
   - `npc_dialogue.json` / `npc_dialogue.ts` (Caretaker, Hermit, Ranger/Trader)
   - `quests.json` / `quests.ts`
   - `items.json` / `items.ts`
   - `location_descriptions.json` / `location_descriptions.ts`
   Goal: **data-driven** so adding a new creature or room doesn’t require touching engine code.
3. **UI Layer (`/src/ui`)**
   React components that render the current state and dispatch actions.
   - `App.tsx`
     - Root component; provides `GameState` via context.
     - Handles initial “New Game” / “Continue” flow.
   - `GameScreen.tsx`
     - Main layout: text log (left), art panel (right), choice/action bar (bottom).
     - Renders HUD (HP, level, XP, current quest snippet).
   - `TextLog.tsx`
     - Renders a list of `LogEntry` objects from state.
     - Auto-scroll behaviour with gentle scrolling.
   - `ActionBar.tsx`
     - Shows context-sensitive buttons:
       - Move options, sense, gather, talk, rest, etc.
     - Optional input line for typed commands.
   - `ArtPanel.tsx`
     - Shows background art for current location.
     - Shows NPC portrait when in dialogue scenes.
   - `HUD.tsx`
     - HP bar, XP bar, level, location name.
     - Simple quest tracker.
   - `SettingsModal.tsx`
     - Text size, theme, audio volume toggles.
4. **Audio Layer (`/src/audio`)**
   - `audioManager.ts`
     - Simple wrapper around Web Audio / Howler.
     - Functions:
       - `playAmbient(trackId)`
       - `stopAmbient()`
       - `playSfx(sfxId)`
       - `playSting(stingId)`
     - Hooks triggered from engine/React via a small adapter.
5. **Platform Layer (`/src/platform`)**
   - `storage.ts`
     - Browser `localStorage` (or IndexedDB later) for save data.
   - `config.ts`
     - Build-time flags (e.g. debug mode, test seed, etc.).
---
### 3.2 Data Flow
- UI dispatches actions ? **Engine** updates `GameState` ? returns new `GameState` + log entries.
- `GameState` stored in React context or a global store (e.g. Zustand).
- UI re-renders based on new `GameState`.
- Persistence layer listens for:
  - “Safe points” (e.g. returning to Sanctum) and auto-saves.
- Audio layer reacts to:
  - Location changes (switch ambient).
  - Events (combat start/end, level up, quest complete).
---
## 4. Constraints & Non-Goals (Vertical Slice)
### 4.1 Constraints
- **Text-first:**  
  - All critical info must be in the text log and simple HUD.
  - Art and audio enhance, but never replace, textual clarity.
- **Light Art:**  
  - Static background per key location.
  - Portraits for Caretaker, Hermit, Ranger/Trader.
  - No heavy animations or particle effects in the slice.
- **Simple Audio:**  
  - 1–2 ambient loops (Sanctum, Wilds cluster).
  - 2–4 UI / feedback SFX (click, hit, level up, quest complete).
  - No dynamic music system yet.
- **Browser-friendly:**  
  - No large frameworks beyond React/TS/Vite.
  - No mandatory WebGL; everything should run on integrated graphics.
### 4.2 Non-Goals (For the Vertical Slice)
These can come *later*:
- Mobile layout and touch controls (nice later, not core now).
- Controller support.
- Multiplayer, online leaderboards, or cloud saves.
- Deep analytics/telemetry.
- Procedural map generation (slice is handcrafted).
---
## 5. Why Browser-Only is Fine for “Indie AAA”
- **Player experience > packaging.**  
  Indie AAA is about **craft**:
  - Strong writing and atmosphere,
  - Solid systems and UX,
  - Cohesive art and sound,
  - Reliability and polish.
- **Web is a mature platform.**  
  Modern browsers can handle:
  - Rich UIs,
  - Audio,
  - Save systems,
  - Plenty of content for a text-first RPG.
- **Future wrapping is easy.**  
  If/when we want:
  - We can use **Electron** or **Tauri** to ship the same code as a desktop app (Steam, Itch downloads) with minimal changes.
For the purposes of Whispering Wilds, a **browser-first, text-first** stack is not a limitation; it’s a strength.  
It plays directly to the game’s identity and makes iteration and testing faster.
